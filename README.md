# DMV Universe

Browser-based city and arcade game. Touch gestures on mobile and mouse drags on
desktop use the same controls. Progress saves in this browser on this device.

## Arcade, business and lounge levels

Basketball, driving, football, soccer, tennis, golf, boxing, pool, air hockey and
chess, Lemonade Stand, Sub Shop, Carryout Craze, Resell Hub, Courier Lane Sprint
and Baseball Diamond Batting
have endless numbered levels. Win a level to unlock the next; select a cleared
level to practice. The panel above each game shows its clear goal and reward.
Tennis retains automatic movement, serving and hitting, with optional swipe aiming.
Air hockey now uses a slower, delayed-reaction CPU, a larger opponent goal,
a larger player mallet, bounded puck speed and automatic goal guarding when
you release the controls. Tap or drag in your half; a gentle contact returns
the puck without a fast flick. Later levels remain harder but keep these assists.

- First clears pay **3, 5, 7, 9 and 12 stars** at levels 1–5, then **12 stars**
  at every later level, once per level per game.
- Cleared levels are **practice only**: replay wins pay **0 stars**.
  There is no daily reward cap or reset to track.
- Losses and chess draws pay **0 stars**. Retrying is free.
- Levels and first clears save alongside existing purchases.
  Saves from before campaigns start at level 1; existing campaign saves keep
  their clears and unlock the next level, including level 6 after five clears.

The first five levels pay 36 stars per game (576 across all sixteen games). Existing
shirts cost 6–8 stars, scooters/e-bikes 60/180, and the Bucket/pickup 720/828. Replaying easy
levels therefore cannot fund purchases.
Existing balances and owned vehicles are preserved; the new prices apply only
to new purchases. The former Impala is displayed as **the Bucket**, retaining
its saved `impala` ID. The pickup costs exactly 15% more and moves 10% faster
(speed multipliers 1.8 versus 1.98).
Starting from zero, buying the Bucket using just one game's
first clears takes 62 wins, and the pickup takes 71 (without other spending).
Across all sixteen games, clearing their first five levels still leaves 144/252
stars to earn toward those vehicles. These are win counts, not guaranteed hours.

The level picker shows a small moving window rather than an ever-growing list.
Use Previous to revisit older levels or Latest unlocked to return to progression.
There is no gameplay level cap. Levels after five use diminishing difficulty
growth toward safe playability limits, rather than endlessly increasing raw
speeds, targets or damage. Goals stay achievable: soccer never requires more than
five of six goals, pool keeps at least eight shots, and tennis flight times stay
positive. Later chess levels reduce evaluation errors without unbounded AI search.

Higher levels increase basketball targets and hoop movement; football defenders;
soccer goals, wall size and keeper reactions; tennis and air hockey opponent
pressure; driving speed and boss strength; boxing stamina and punch speed; and
chess accuracy/lookahead. Pool has fewer shots; golf requires fewer strokes,
softer cup entry and faster windmill timing.

The five business/street games use the same touch canvas, visual feedback,
level picker and first-clear rewards as the sports games. Older ten-game saves
keep their clears and initialize the five additions at level one.
Existing fifteen-game saves also gain baseball at level one without losing progress.
Arcades fill the viewport on mobile; level-selection controls hide during play
and return with the result, leaving more room for touch controls.

- **Lemonade Stand**: squeeze, mix, hold/release to pour, set prices, serve and
  restock from sales. Meet both cup and profit targets; stand cash is separate
  from city stars.
- **Sub Shop**: assemble the exact visible ingredient ticket before patience
  expires. Toggle ingredients to remove extras; remakes cost patience.
- **Carryout Craze**: time the fryer, choose the requested sauce and pack wing
  boxes. The house-special menu, sauce sample and glazed wings show mambo sauce
  as glossy dark red to lighter red, ketchup-like rather than orange. The timing
  is fictional arcade play, not cooking instruction.
- **Resell Hub**: compare three details against a reference and buy matching
  items or pass mismatches. Fictional sneakers are not real authentication
  training; displayed resale margin is practice money only.
- **Courier Lane Sprint**: steer between three lanes, pick up parcels, avoid
  cones and deliver at green zones. Three bumps end the route.
- **Baseball Diamond Batting**: tap SWING as the pitch reaches the green home-plate
  ring or green timing-bar zone. Hit 4 of 10 pitches initially; closer timing
  earns a home-run animation. Later levels need up to 6 hits with faster pitches
  and narrower but bounded windows. Canceled touches do not swing.

Food and resale graphics include shaded lemonade glasses with liquid and ice,
sliced lemons, layered seeded subs, textured wings with fries, ingredient
previews and detailed fictional sneakers with stitching, laces and soles.

This is a local, client-side economy, not a tamper-proof online wallet. Clearing
browser storage resets progress; editing saves or the device clock cannot be
securely prevented without server-side accounts and reward validation.

## Quadrant neighborhoods and streets

NW has leafy rowhouse blocks and a cultural district; NE has a residential grid
separate from its market, carryouts and sports; SE has garden-apartment courts
and library commons; SW has waterfront homes beside civic and arena areas.
All existing venues remain in their quadrants. Every home, venue, neighborhood
shop and sports destination has an obstacle-free local street approach joining
the main crossroads. Local streets use the main road's asphalt, sidewalks and
yellow markings at a narrower neighborhood scale. Trees stay off the streets.
Neighborhood homes open the same saved home/computer/TV menu as The Crib.
Each residential type now has three distinct silhouettes: bay-front, L-shaped
and porch/setback houses; L-wing, stepped-tower and courtyard condos; slab,
setback and twin-block apartments. Pitched/hipped roofs, terraces,
balcony railings, planters and varied window bands distinguish them. Homes
and condos have visible driveways, some with decorative parked cars or a
small garage; apartment lots have **4–6 individually marked spaces**, wheel
stops, an access aisle and several detailed parked cars. The main apartment's
garage shares its full rear wall, with side-facing roll-up doors and a physical
**PARK HERE / GARAGE** board mounted on the garage wall. Apartment fronts have
stone waterfall walls, animated falling water, catch pools and landscaped
garden islands, separate from the parking bays. Selected
houses carry hanging **FOR SALE** boards on cross-arm posts; apartments and
condos advertise **1–4 BEDROOMS AVAILABLE** on framed, twin-post leasing
boards. Sign panels have physical depth and lettering projected onto their
isometric faces, not floating screen labels. These are world-building signs, not new property
purchases or separate bedroom inventories. Parking cars are decorative;
the existing saved vehicle/garage menu is unchanged.
Apartments use broad 160×110 lots and condos 140×100, compared with 80×60
rowhouses and the main apartment's 170×130 footprint. Their heights remain
proportional rather than stretching into thin towers. Apartment courts are
spaced around the library commons and southern lots are repositioned to keep
the larger properties inside the map; local streets
and interaction approaches are rebuilt around their collision footprints.
All balconies, driveways and parking details stay within their property lots.

The city uses consistent **compact arcade proportions**, not real-world map
distances. All sedans, whether drivable, in a driveway or in a parking lot,
share one 28×14 footprint, 18-unit roof height and the same detailed renderer.
Pickups are only slightly larger at 32×16; two-wheelers are 23–25 units long.
Parking bays, collision bounds and owned rides use that same scale.
For-sale houses have empty driveways/garages; parked cars appear only at
occupied houses. Basketball courts (150×140) are smaller than tennis courts
(180×170), which are smaller than baseball diamonds and football/soccer
grounds. Houses remain smaller than apartment blocks and major venues.

The city map shows the local streets, tan home markers, blue neighborhood-shop
markers and numbered venues, with descriptions of each district.
Baseball has a standalone grass-and-dirt diamond in SW's open block south of
the main street (compass center east 520, south 1430), with bases, foul lines,
foul poles and a street approach. Its placement is temporary and can be changed
in `centralLayouts.SW.activities` in `index.html`. The fan hub and arena also
launch the same saved baseball campaign.

## Interactive interiors and appearance

Entering a building changes the backdrop and opens a themed room. Tap the
illustrated objects or use keyboard Tab/Enter; these are the same working
activity buttons, not decorative screenshots. Home has a TV, computer, wardrobe
and garage. The library has books, a public computer, movies and a gaming shelf.
The club has a stage, pool, air hockey and chess; the mall has merchandise and
dispatch counters. Food businesses and the boxing gym open an interior before
you start their game. The arena has a concourse and a practice-lab terminal.
Other public venues retain their existing activities inside themed surroundings.

The waterfront arena has an oval exterior with segmented glass, a curved roof
and entrance canopies. Collision and street clearances still use its existing
rectangular footprint, so visual corners do not become new shortcuts.
Avatars retain their saved choices, with additional hair texture, facial-hair
shaping, eyes, eyebrows, skin shading, clothing seams and sneaker details.
Beards now follow the jaw as a narrow outline with a small mustache and a visible
mouth; shirts use one continuous torso-and-sleeve silhouette without a layered
chest panel or second hem.

Every arcade entrance has original game-specific venue artwork, city landmarks
and framed **Legendary 202** tribute portraits. A compact portrait strip remains
visible during play without covering the game's touch targets. Real DC/DMV
athletes and cultural figures are identified by name and local association.
These are stylized original interpretations, not photographs, licensed likeness
claims or endorsements. Building interiors also have original Chocolate City
skyline/rowhouse murals and named tribute galleries; no external image service
is used. `city-art.js` centralizes the palettes, portraits and venue themes.

## Casino and scheduled promos

The **Chocolate City Casino** occupies the open northwest block of NE (compass
center east -570, south 210), with its own connected street and interactive
tables. It includes the existing sports practice lab plus blackjack, pass-line
craps, European single-zero roulette and a three-reel slot machine.
Casino practice begins with 1,000 credits, separate from city stars and from
the sportsbook's practice balance. Each table displays its rules and payouts.
There are no deposits, cash prizes or credit-to-star conversions.
An unfinished blackjack hand or craps point is saved with its already-deducted
stake; returning or reloading never refunds or charges it again. Finish that
round before using another casino table. An exhausted practice wallet can be
refilled for free; event chips cannot.

Optional announced promos cost **5 stars for one entry with 1,000 event chips**.
One entry per event, no rebuys and no conversion back into stars. Event chips
remain separate from practice credits. New bets stop at the event's closing
time; an existing hand or point can still finish. **No dates are enabled yet.**
To announce one later, add an entry to `CasinoEvents.schedule` in
`casino-events.js` with a unique lowercase `id`, a `title`, and ISO `startsAt` /
`endsAt` timestamps including `Z` or an explicit timezone offset. Never reuse
an event ID. The UI displays Washington, DC time; daylight-saving offsets must
be correct for the chosen date. Scheduling and entries use local saves and the
device clock, not a tamper-proof server.

## Pet profiles and companions

All five pet types and pet items are available for economy testing. Adopt one
home-habitat pet (fish **5 stars**, turtle **8**) plus one walking companion
(rabbit **10**, cat **12**, dog **15**). These are provisional prices, shown
before purchase and configured in `Pets.settings.prices` in `pets.js`.
Cats, dogs and rabbits follow you on foot between quadrants, avoid collision
boundaries and appear in your home pet area. They are hidden while riding.
Fish and turtles stay in their illustrated home habitats.

The home pet object opens a saved profile with a 1–24-character name, food,
fun, affection and energy indicators, feeding, play/enrichment, attention,
rest, treats and training. Fish/turtles use quiet watching and target
enrichment rather than petting or dog tricks. Four activities unlock at
30-point training intervals; care has a ten-second cooldown. No illness,
death, star production or permanent offline penalties are modeled.
Collars, bandanas, toys, beds, plants, habitat hideaways, decor and treats cost
the displayed stars; owned items can be equipped or put away. Starter adoption
and item purchases are never free. Pets, inventory and purchases share the
normal browser save.

## Betting Practice Lab

Open an arena’s **Betting Practice Lab** to learn with a separate 1,000-credit
practice balance. No real money, deposits, prizes or conversion to city stars.
Matchups and prices are fixed examples; scores are simulated, not live data.

The mobile-friendly Practice screen walks through matchup, market and bet slip.
Moneyline, spread, totals and player props explain what needs to happen. The
slip separates stake, net profit, total return, full loss risk and price-implied
probability. Select one pick per game; two games make a parlay, while independent
singles can be placed separately. Stakes remain entered when switching screens.

The simulator stays above the educational material. **Learning sections**
jumps to an index below it: start here, odds, price-implied chance/house edge,
bet types, singles/parlays/pushes, stake/profit/risk and real-app differences.
Only one short lesson appears at a time; a selector switches topics without
clearing picks or the stake. A separate glossary defines common words, and
the quick quiz shows one question at a time. The guided example spends no
credits until the player explicitly places the ticket.
Lesson paragraphs are boxed into separate note cards, with a visible divider
and space between individual sentences. Numbered starting steps each get their
own card. Market explainers, glossary definitions and quiz feedback also use
sentence-level spacing without breaking decimal odds.
**Tickets** shows pending stakes, simulated scores and numerical explanations of
wins, losses and pushes. Existing simulator balances and tickets are retained.
Resetting practice data does not affect city progress. Practice results are not
betting advice or evidence of real-money profitability.

## Home computer, investing and the SE library

The **Personal Computer** in The Crib / apartment menu opens an **Investing
Practice Lab**, separate from city stars and betting credits. The new **SE Public
Library** also has public computers accessing that same saved portfolio. Both
locations include a Back to computer route; neither requires a purchase.

Start with 10,000 practice dollars. Explore two fictional stocks, a broad-market
ETF, a balanced mutual fund, a fixed-coupon bond and fully paid calls/puts.
Review each order's cost, quantity and risks before placing it. Stocks, ETFs,
bonds and options fill at displayed synthetic quotes. Mutual fund orders wait for
the next closing NAV and explicitly reject buys if cash is then insufficient;
they reserve no cash and can be canceled before filling. Units are whole numbers.
No short selling, margin or uncovered options are available.

Advance one simulated day manually to move prices and process orders. The model
includes fund expenses accrued inside NAV, quarterly cash distributions with
matching price reductions, a 360-day bond with semiannual coupons and principal
repayment, and 30-day options with a 100-share contract multiplier. Options
cash-settle intrinsic value at expiry; fresh contracts then become available.
This intentionally differs from physical exercise in many real equity options.
The portfolio shows cash, marked holdings, cost basis, realized/unrealized results,
income, weights and the latest 60 daily closing snapshots. Lessons and quizzes
explain diversification, compounding, expenses, rates, option risk and real-app
differences. Synthetic prices, option time value and market news are teaching
examples, not live data or valuation advice. Taxes, inflation, trading spreads,
liquidity and issuer default are not modeled.

The library's **Short Reads** offers six original standalone fiction stories,
each 400–800 words (roughly 2–4 minutes at 200 words/minute). Three are featured
each week; the shelf moves forward every Monday at 00:00 UTC and repeats after
six weeks. All six remain browsable. This rotates a bundled catalog rather than
claiming to generate new writing automatically. Reading and practice trading
never award stars. Old saves initialize the investing lab without altering
existing balances, purchases or minigame clears.

## Library shelves, documentaries, TV, theater and the DMV

`library-data.js` holds 141 short, neutral **fact cards**, each with a source
link. Generated content only selects and orders those cards; it never writes new
historical claims. To add content, add a cited card and reuse an existing
category.

Source links are further reading, not independent verification. This collection
still needs a complete editorial fact-check; its in-game notice says so. Study
current agency manuals for driving, licensing and safety rules. Historical
summaries are not current news, professional training or legal advice.

- **Bookshelves** (SE Library) have six aisles:
  - **Chocolate City Collection**: go-go; art, style and scenes; the hard years,
    with a content note; sports; entertainment; Black politics.
  - **Early-2000s DC News Archive**: headlines and entertainment, presented as
    history rather than live news.
  - **History & Education**: African American history, Native American history,
    DC through Black Americans and DMV regional history.
  - **Sports**.
  - **Modern Entertainment Archive**: separate music, sports and TV/film
    shelves with 18 dated events from **2021–2024**. This curated archive
    includes original summaries and source links, not current headlines,
    a live feed or automatic news fetching.
  - **Video Games: History & Development**: early interactive experiments,
    arcades, home consoles, 3D and independent games, plus prototypes, game
    loops, visual art, audio, testing and accessibility.

  Each shelf rotates deterministic editions every Monday at 00:00 UTC. Books
  contain 400–800 words (2–4 minutes at 200 words/minute), including active-recall
  and source-evaluation prompts, and list their sources. This is a finite
  catalog: chapters can repeat between editions.
- **Video Games Reading Room**: four original, approximately one-minute briefs
  about dated 2023–2025 launches, design and accessibility. Each links to further
  reading. These are newly added summaries of archived events, not live 2026
  reporting or copied publisher articles. Current source sites could not be
  fetched during this pass; editorial verification is still pending.
- **Fictional Gossip Magazine Rack**: six original short comedy stories from
  invented magazines, with absurd appliance, raccoon, elevator, fruit, sneaker
  and game-character drama. Every screen labels the stories as fictional satire,
  not real celebrity gossip. Both racks feature two entries each UTC week
  from a finite catalog; the complete catalog remains available.
- **Movie room** (SE Library): nine original 2–4 minute documentaries are rebuilt
  weekly as narrated scene slides, with optional browser text-to-speech. A
  real-film guide offers original summaries and source links; nothing is streamed.
  YouTube embeds, hosted video and device-selected video files are not implemented.
  Playback supports pause, back, next and replay. Reading players pause when
  the tab is hidden; narration resumes when playback does, and unavailable
  browser speech is reported on screen.
- **📺 Living Room TV** (Crib menu): nine channels play random clips from each
  channel's categories, with channel up and down controls. Clips do not repeat
  immediately; Back revisits the recent clip history.
- **U Street Picture House** (NW): five longer feature documentaries plus two
  occasional **live performance slots** run on one shared UTC schedule. The music
  showcase and mini-play are original, explicitly fictional illustrated previews,
  not live feeds, recordings or musical audio. They reserve slots for later
  performances. Devices with accurate
  clocks see the same scene; this is not a network synchronization service.
  Features are separated by 60-second intermissions, and the theater shows an
  upcoming schedule in local time. Pause to read freezes only your screen;
  Rejoin returns to the current shared scene. Optional browser narration is
  available. Film screenings have a glowing cinema screen and audience seats;
  performance slots switch the auditorium and backdrop to velvet curtains,
  a proscenium, stage lighting and a wood stage. Pausing preserves the current
  scene and theme; rejoining updates both to the current program. Intermissions
  prepare the next show's theme. Edit `theater-program.js` to add original stage
  previews: give each event a unique `id`, `title`, `icon`, `kind` (`music` or
  `play`), `afterFeature` matching a documentary's ID, and original `scenes` with
  `title`, `narration` and positive `seconds`. Actual video/audio performance
  playback still needs a separate media integration.
  Audience messages are explicitly labeled NPC or You. Chat is
  local to the current visit, capped at 140 characters per message and 30
  messages, and never sent to other players. No chat server is used.
- **🧰 Trades & careers shelf** (`learning-data.js`): 12 plain-language study
  guides for plumbing, electrical, construction, HVAC, IT support,
  cybersecurity, welding, auto, CDL, CNA, culinary, barbering and cosmetology.
  Each has a memory trick, safety awareness, the path in, and links to BLS for
  dated pay and outlook figures rather than unverified salary claims. Hazardous
  tasks require approved training and supervision. The **Skills Quiz Center**
  on any home or library computer gives five-question quizzes, saves best
  scores and lets you review all answers after finishing.
- **DMV Service Center** (SW): learner-permit practice tests for DC, MD and VA.
  Each test has 10 original questions: seven from the chosen jurisdiction and
  three jurisdiction-neutral traffic-sign/signal questions. Other states'
  laws are never mixed in. Choices are shuffled. A study-first screen and
  post-test answer review include explanations and official study links.
  Scoring 80% records an in-game practice permit, not a real permit. The short
  quizzes do not replicate official exam lengths, sections or pass requirements.

Sensitive topics carry content notes in books and media, not just on the
crime shelf. Learning screens reset scroll and keyboard focus on navigation;
Tab stays within the activity dialog. Media timers and narration are stopped
when the player leaves or closes the activity.

Saves add an optional, validated `learning` section: `{ quizBest, permits }`.
Old saves still load unchanged. None of these features award stars.
